\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Masked diffusion for language and speech.}
Discrete diffusion with an absorbing mask state \citep{d3pm} was later simplified and improved
\citep{sedd,mdlm,md4}, given a scaling law \citep{nie2025scaling} and trained at 8B
parameters \citep{llada}. These studies scale training compute and treat sampling steps as
a trade-off between speed and quality.
SoundStorm \citep{soundstorm} brought MaskGIT confidence decoding \citep{maskgit} to residual
codebooks, decoded one level at a time as in our sampler. MaskGCT \citep{maskgct},
NaturalSpeech~3 \citep{naturalspeech3} and recent masked or block diffusion models
\citep{fan2026lladatts,madha2026dllmtts} also generate discrete speech tokens by masked
prediction. Flow matching \citep{voicebox,e2tts,f5tts} is the main continuous alternative, and
autoregressive models \citep{audiolm,valle,seedtts,cosyvoice,llasa} emit tokens left to
right; their token stage has no refinement steps, although Seed-TTS and CosyVoice render the
tokens with a diffusion or flow-matching decoder. Step ablations exist. SoundStorm finds that audio quality stops
improving past its default number of iterations, F5-TTS reports WER and SIM-o at two NFE
settings, and MaskGCT notes that WER improves far more than SIM-o over its step sweep. Each
covers a few operating points of one model, and raw changes in WER and SIM-o have no common
unit. We measure both as the share of the distance to real audio that refinement closes,
across many shapes at equal parameter count.

\paragraph{Test-time compute: refinement and search.}
Language models often spend test-time compute by sampling candidates and keeping the one a
verifier prefers \citep{cobbe2021verifiers}. At matched FLOPs this can replace a much larger
model on problems the small model sometimes solves, but not on the hardest ones
\citep{testtime}. In continuous diffusion, gains from more denoising steps flatten after a
few dozen steps, while verifier-guided search over the sampling noise keeps improving samples
\citep{ma2025inference}. Masked diffusion commits tokens for good. ReMDM adds remasking so
that extra steps keep helping \citep{remdm}, and UnMaskFork searches over unmasking
trajectories \citep{misaki2026unmaskfork}. In TTS, Llasa finds that beam search on a
speaker-similarity verifier raises SIM but can leave WER worse than without search, unless a
WER verifier is added \citep{llasa}, and best-of-$N$ gains
depend on whether the verifier and the evaluating ASR share a model family
\citep{yu2026bonconfound}. We compare refinement and search in one sampler without
remasking, at equal NFE, and test whether the search gain depends on the selecting and
scoring speaker encoders sharing an architecture (\S\ref{sec:search}).

\paragraph{Depth, width and iterated depth.}
At a fixed non-embedding parameter count, loss depends only weakly on transformer shape
\citep{kaplan}, and compute-optimal scaling is stated in parameters and tokens
\citep{chinchilla}. Yet depth beats width only below a width-dependent threshold
\citep{levine2020depth}, downstream quality depends on shape when pre-training loss does
not \citep{tay2022scale}, and deeper models of equal size generalise more compositionally,
with diminishing returns \citep{petty2024depth}. Weight-tied recurrence trades parameters for iterations
\citep{dehghani2019universal,saunshi2025looped,geiping2025recurrent}, and in masked diffusion,
recursive depth can partly replace denoising steps \citep{carballo2026recursive}. In our models,
layers do not share weights and each refinement step fixes some tokens for good, so a step is
not simply another pass through the same layers. We could not identify a fixed exchange rate
between depth and steps (\S\ref{sec:scope}).
