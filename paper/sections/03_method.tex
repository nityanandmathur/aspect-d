\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Model and training.}
The generator is a bidirectional pre-LN transformer \citep{preln} over the 8 coarsest residual
codebooks of the Mimi codec \citep{moshi} at 12.5\,Hz. A model has width $w$, depth $d$, head
dimension 64, a GELU MLP of ratio 4 \citep{gelu}, RMSNorm \citep{rmsnorm}, RoPE \citep{rope}
and no weight tying, so its non-embedding parameter count is $N = 12\,d\,w^{2}$
\citep{kaplan}. Training uses the coarse-to-fine masking of SoundStorm \citep{soundstorm}:
each example masks part of one random level and all finer levels, and the loss is
cross-entropy on the masked cells of that level. There is no separate
speaker encoder and no classifier-free guidance (CFG) \citep{cfg}. The target length is the
number of characters in the target text times \Nsecperchar\,s, the median duration per
character of training clips.

\paragraph{Refinement steps and search.}
Inference uses MaskGIT confidence decoding \citep{maskgit}, one codebook level at a time from
coarse to fine, with the same number of \emph{refinement steps} $T$ per level. A sample
therefore costs NFE $=8T$, where NFE is the number of generator forward passes per
utterance. Each step predicts all masked cells, samples tokens at temperature 1 and commits
the most confident ones; committed cells are never masked again. The cosine schedule, the
Gumbel confidence noise and its seeds are fixed across models and $T$. The \emph{default} is $T{=}16$.
\emph{Best-of-$K$ search} draws $K$ candidates at $T{=}8$ each (NFE $=64K$), and a
\emph{selector} keeps the candidate whose speaker embedding is closest to the prompt. At
\emph{matched NFE} it is compared with refinement at $T{=}8K$: NFE 128, 256 and 512 for
$K{=}2,4,8$. Its \emph{selection cost} is the compute it adds on top of the generator:
one Mimi decode and one selector embedding per candidate.

\paragraph{Metrics and reference points.}
Intelligibility is WER from the automatic speech recognition (ASR) model Whisper-large-v3
\citep{whisper} with greedy decoding, checked by a wav2vec~2.0 CTC recogniser
\citep{wav2vec2,xu2021selftraining}. Speaker identity is SIM-o
\citep{voicebox}, the cosine similarity of speaker embeddings of the output and the original
prompt recording. Its encoder, \emph{WavLM-L}, is the seed-tts-eval verification model
\citep{seedtts}: WavLM-large \citep{wavlm} with an ECAPA-TDNN-small head \citep{ecapa}.
Because WavLM-L also picks the search candidate, we score search with a second encoder,
\emph{ECAPA} (the SpeechBrain \citep{speechbrain} ECAPA-TDNN \citep{ecapa} trained on VoxCeleb
\citep{voxceleb1,voxceleb2}).
In a swapped control, ECAPA selects and WavLM-L scores. As both use an ECAPA-TDNN head, we
also rescore search with an x-vector \citep{xvector}, a GE2E d-vector \citep{ge2e} and
WavLM-base+ \citep{wavlm} (App.~\ref{app:search}). ECAPA and the recognisers are used only
for evaluation. Naturalness is UTMOS \citep{utmos}. An output is \emph{degenerate} if
synthesis failed or its transcript is empty, has under 30\% of the reference characters, or
repeats one 4-gram six or more times in a row. Each metric is averaged over all items,
degenerate ones included. The \emph{WER floor} is Whisper's WER on the real target recordings
(\NwerFloor). The \emph{SIM-o ceiling} is the SIM-o of the real target after a Mimi round
trip (\NsimCeiling). Without the round trip, the real target scores SIM-o \NsimRealAudio;
the difference is what the codec loses. Both reference points are properties of our setup
(real recordings under Whisper, and the Mimi codec), not hard limits: F5-TTS v1 Base
\citep{f5tts}, which does not use Mimi, goes past both. At NFE 32 on our items it reaches WER \NcrsFfWER\
(\NcrsFfWERasrTwo\ under the second ASR), SIM-o \NcrsFfSIM\ and UTMOS \NcrsFfUTMOS. Because
it differs from our models in data, size and output representation, we report it only as a
reference for what a strong system scores on our items (a \emph{calibration anchor}), not as
a system to beat.

\paragraph{Data, design and statistics.}
Models are trained on a \NtrainHours\,h speaker-stratified subset of Emilia-EN \citep{emilia}
with \Nspeakers\ speakers. We evaluate cross-sentence zero-shot synthesis: prompt and target
are different utterances of one speaker. The \NevalItems\ test items come from
\NevalSpeakers\ of \NheldoutSpk\ held-out speakers, with 4--15\,s targets whose real
recording Whisper transcribes at WER at most 0.25. The grid has \Nconfigs\ configurations:
three parameter budgets with five \emph{shapes} (width--depth splits at fixed $N$) each
(App.~\ref{app:grid}), trained at \Nseeds\ seeds for 30k steps with $\mu$P \citep{mup}
(effective batch 256, base learning rate \Nbaselr). We vary $T \in \{\NTgrid\}$ at evaluation
only, which gives \Nsurface\ points per metric from \Nruns\ models; $T{=}32$ and $64$ were
added later for every run (Table~\ref{tab:gapclosed}, App.~\ref{app:surface}) and are not
part of the registered fit. Let the error $e$ be WER
or $1-$SIM-o, averaged over items and runs, and $e^{*}$ the floor or one minus the ceiling.
The \emph{reachable range} runs from $e(1)$ to $e^{*}$, and refinement to $T$ steps closes
the share $s(T) = \bigl(e(1)-e(T)\bigr)/\bigl(e(1)-e^{*}\bigr)$ of it. The \emph{ratio} is
$s_{\mathrm{WER}}/s_{\mathrm{SIM}}$. All intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals
\citep{efron1979bootstrap} over \Nbootreps\ replicates. Unless a caption says otherwise, they
resample runs, because the $T$ points of a run share a model.
