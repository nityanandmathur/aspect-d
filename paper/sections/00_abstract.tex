\begin{abstract}
We ask what more refinement steps and search improve in masked-diffusion text-to-speech
(TTS). We train \Nconfigs\ width--depth shapes (\Nseeds\ seeds each,
\NminN--\NmaxN\,M non-embedding parameters) on \NtrainHours\,h of English speech and vary
the refinement steps $T$ only at inference, testing on \NevalItems\ zero-shot items from
\NevalSpeakers\ held-out speakers. From unintelligible one-step output ($T{=}1$, word error
rate (WER) \Nwertone\%) to the default $T{=}16$, refinement closes \NgapWER\% of the WER gap
to real recordings but only \NgapSIM\% of the gap in speaker similarity, a ratio of
\NgapRatio. On \NtrendRuns\ retrained runs the ratio shrinks with training
(\NgapThirtykRatio, \NgapNinetykRatio\ and \NgapEightykRatio\ at 30k, 90k and 180k steps) and
stays above one at all three lengths. At the same 128 generator passes, keeping the better
of two samples, as judged by one speaker encoder, beats one $T{=}16$ sample by \NcrMatchLod\
in similarity under another encoder and wins on \NcrMatchLowin\% of items; with the encoders
swapped the gain is \NcrXEWLod, against \NtrainGainSim\ from $3\times$ longer training.
Search costs \NcrLedgerSearchWER\ absolute WER and \NcrCostTwopct\% extra compute. The codec
loses \NcodecShare\% of the similarity gap between our best 90k-step model and real audio. We
claim only that refinement helps intelligibility more than speaker identity, for small
models, one English corpus, one codec and automatic metrics.
\end{abstract}
