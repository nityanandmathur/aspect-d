\section{Results}
\subsection{Refinement closes more of the intelligibility range}
\label{sec:gapclosed}
Refinement from $T{=}1$ to the default $T{=}16$ closes \NgapWER\% (CI \NgapWERci) of the
reachable intelligibility range and \NgapSIM\% (CI \NgapSIMci) of the reachable identity
range (Table~\ref{tab:gapclosed}). The ratio of the two shares is \NgapRatio\ (CI
\NgapRatioci). The ranges end at the measured WER floor and SIM-o ceiling
(\S\ref{sec:method}), and no model is fitted.

  \centering
  \caption{\emph{Share of the reachable range closed by refinement}, from $T{=}1$ to each
  $T$, over all \Nruns\ runs at 30k steps. Brackets: 95\% intervals from a bootstrap over runs.}
  \label{tab:gapclosed}
  \small
  \input{tab_gapclosed}
\end{table}

The ratio is stable in $T$: from $T{=}4$ to $T{=}64$ it stays between \NgapRatioTLo\ and
\NgapRatioTHi. It is also stable under monotone maps of the error. With the
original error and under $\log(1+\mathrm{err})$, $\sqrt{\mathrm{err}}$ and $\mathrm{err}^{2}$
it stays between \NgapRatioLo\ and \NgapRatioHi\ (Table~\ref{tab:scope}). The registered
fit's main statistic, the difference $\Delta\tau$ between the two step exponents, is not: it
changes sign under some of these maps (\S\ref{sec:scope}).

At $T{=}1$ almost every output is unintelligible: degenerate outputs have WER
\NcrsWerToneDegen\ and the others \NcrsWerToneNondegen\ (insertions push WER above one).
Degenerate outputs make up \NcrsDegenShareTone\% of outputs at $T{=}1$ and supply
\NcrsDropFromDegen\% of the WER drop to $T{=}16$. Dropping them therefore cannot show
whether this poor start inflates the WER share; starting the count later can
(App.~\ref{app:share}). From $T{=}2$, refinement closes \NcrsFromTwoWER\% of the remaining
intelligibility range and \NcrsFromTwoSIM\% of the identity range, a ratio of
\NcrsFromTwoRatio\ (CI \NcrsFromTwoRatioci); from $T{=}4$ the ratio is \NcrsFromFourRatio.
These are shares of smaller ranges, so only their ordering compares with
Table~\ref{tab:gapclosed}. Keeping only items that are non-degenerate at every $T$
(\NcrsNdAllTItems\ per run on average) gives \NcrsNdAllTRatio\ (CI \NcrsNdAllTRatioci). In each
of the \NcrsOrderingVariants\ variants of Table~\ref{tab:share}a above the F5-TTS row, at 30k
and, where computed, at 90k steps, the intelligibility share is the larger one and the 95\%
interval on the ratio lies above one.

The ordering also holds with F5-TTS, the calibration anchor of \S\ref{sec:method}, as the
bound. With its WER as the floor and its SIM-o as the ceiling, the shares are
\NcrsFfBoundWER\% and \NcrsFfBoundSIM\%, a ratio of \NcrsFfBoundRatio\ (CI
\NcrsFfBoundRatioci). F5-TTS does not emit Mimi tokens, so its SIM-o is not limited by our
codec.

\subsection{The ratio shrinks with training}
\label{sec:trend}
Training longer shrinks the ratio, and the ordering holds at every length we tested
(Table~\ref{tab:trend}). We retrained \NtrendRuns\ runs (C1, C3 and C5, \Nseeds\ seeds each)
to 90k and 180k steps. The ratio falls from \NgapThirtykRatio\ at 30k steps to
\NgapNinetykRatio\ at 90k and \NgapEightykRatio\ at 180k, and every interval lies above one.
Longer training here means more passes over the same \NtrainHours\,h of speech, with no new
data: \NcrsEpochsThirtyk, \NcrsEpochsNinetyk\ and \NcrsEpochsEightyk\ passes.

  \label{tab:trend}

The intelligibility share reaches a plateau across training; the identity share does not.
From 90k to 180k steps the intelligibility share changes by $\NsatWER$ points under Whisper
and $\NsatWERasrTwo$ points under the second recogniser, a CTC model of another family. The
identity share rises from \NgapNinetykSIM\% to \NgapEightykSIM\%, so the ratio falls. Because
the intelligibility share is already \NgapNinetykWER\% at 90k steps, further training can
only move the ratio toward one; the ordering at 180k says that refinement leaves more of the
identity range open, not that the gap between the two shares stays large.

The ratio is also smaller at the largest budget. At 30k steps it is \NcrsBudgetARatio\ (CI
\NcrsBudgetARatioci) at 20\,M, \NcrsBudgetBRatio\ and \NcrsBudgetCRatio\ at 50 and 125\,M,
and \NcrsBudgetDRatio\ (CI \NcrsBudgetDRatioci) at \NmaxNfour\,M, which has two seeds per
shape (Table~\ref{tab:share}b). Prompt CFG also shrinks it (\S\ref{sec:limits}). We claim
the ordering, which holds at every budget and training length we tested. Three training
lengths do not show where the ratio converges, so we do not extrapolate it.
