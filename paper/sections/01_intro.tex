\section{Introduction}
A diffusion language model can spend serial compute in two ways: through its depth $d$,
fixed in training and paid on every forward pass, or through its refinement steps $T$,
chosen per request at inference. A standard autoregressive transformer has only the first.
If extra steps could do the work of extra layers, a smaller model could be served with more
steps, but only for the qualities that more steps improve.

Zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) has two capabilities that need not improve at the same rate:
intelligibility, measured by word error rate (WER), and speaker identity, measured by speaker
similarity to the original prompt (SIM-o). We train one grid of masked-diffusion codec TTS
models \citep{soundstorm,maskgct}. In each of three parameter budgets, the shapes split a fixed
parameter count differently between width and depth, with \Nseeds\ seeds each. We vary $T$
only at inference and compare each metric with its value on real recordings
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

Refinement mostly fixes the words. From $T{=}1$ to the default $T{=}16$ it closes \NgapWER\%
of the reachable WER range and \NgapSIM\% of the reachable SIM-o range, a ratio of
\NgapRatio\ (\S\ref{sec:gapclosed}). At $T{=}1$ the output is unintelligible, and the
ordering also holds when counting starts at $T{=}2$ (\NcrsFromTwoRatio) or $T{=}4$
(\NcrsFromFourRatio). On \NtrendRuns\
retrained runs the ratio shrinks with training but stays above one: \NgapThirtykRatio,
\NgapNinetykRatio\ and \NgapEightykRatio\ at 30k, 90k and 180k steps (\S\ref{sec:trend}).

Search does raise identity. With the same 128 generator forward passes (matched NFE),
best-of-2 search beats $T{=}16$ in speaker similarity by \NcrMatchLod\ when one encoder
(WavLM-L) selects and another (ECAPA) scores, and by \NcrXEWLod\ with the roles swapped
(Table~\ref{tab:search}). It costs \NcrLedgerSearchWER\ absolute WER and \NcrCostTwopct\%
extra compute for selection.

Before training, we pre-registered four hypotheses about a power law fitted to the error as
a function of width, depth and $T$, two of them declared primary (App.~\ref{app:prereg}).
The first primary hypothesis, that WER falls faster with $T$ than identity error, passed its
registered test, but the tested quantity changes sign when the error is rescaled, for
example by a square root (\S\ref{sec:scope}). The second, a fixed exchange rate between
depth and steps, failed its registered criterion, and the exponent that would set it sits at
the limit set for the fit, so we could not identify an exchange rate. The shape hypothesis
could not be decided, because the coefficient of the width term also hits its limit, and the
prediction from small to large budgets failed. Our main measurement, the share of the reachable range, was added after the
registered analysis and is not a pre-registered test.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}\hspace{0.4em}\cnum{1}~A seeded grid of \Nruns\
masked-diffusion TTS models with $T$ varied at inference (\Nsurface\ points per metric),
released with code and run records (App.~\ref{app:repro}).
\cnum{2}~A measure that needs no fitted model: for each metric, the share of the distance to
real audio that refinement closes, checked from later starting steps, without failed outputs
and after longer training.
\cnum{3}~A comparison, in one unit (SIM-o under WavLM-L), of what raises speaker identity
beyond the default $T{=}16$: longer training raises it most, then more parameters
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}). At the same NFE, search gains about twice as much as
speaker-contrastive guidance (\NcrXEWMidd\ against \NcrGuideGainSim\ at NFE 256), and its
gain grows with more candidates.

These results come from one small regime. Models have \NminN--\NmaxN\,M non-embedding
parameters, plus one \NmaxNfour\,M budget, and train for 30k steps on \NtrainHours\,h of
English from one corpus \citep{emilia}; the 90k and 180k runs repeat the same data. We use
one codec, one sampler, no classifier-free guidance in the main grid, and automatic metrics
only. Our best 90k-step model reaches SIM-o \NcrsNinetykBestSIM, below the SIM-o
ceiling (\NsimCeiling) and below F5-TTS \citep{f5tts} on the same items (\NcrsFfSIM). We claim the
ordering of the two shares within this regime only.
